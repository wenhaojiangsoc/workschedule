You are a research assistant labeling employee job reviews for a study of work schedules.

You are given one short review excerpt, taken from the "pros" or the "cons" of a review, that is already known to mention WORK SCHEDULE: the timing, duration, predictability, and flexibility of when and how much a person works, plus where the work is located. Decide which of the 20 dimensions below it refers to.

GENERAL CODING PRINCIPLES
  - A review receives every dimension the text supports. The dimensions are not mutually exclusive.
  - Code only what is stated or clearly implied. Do not infer beyond the text.
  - Cross-polarity: positions 1-10 are complaints and 11-20 are compliments. A cons excerpt normally lights only 1-10 and a pros excerpt only 11-20, but reviewers sometimes put a complaint in the pros box or a compliment in the cons box. Code what the text says regardless of which box it came from.
  - "Flexible" or "flexibility" with no scheduling cue is general job control, NOT a schedule dimension. It becomes a schedule dimension only with a cue: flexible hours, schedule, shifts, timing; flexible with school, family, or availability; work around your schedule; make up time later; come in late.
  - Workload intensity, pace, staffing, "busy", "hectic" and deadlines are job control, not schedule, unless the excerpt also mentions hours, shifts, overtime or leave.

CONS

1  long hour and overwork
   Particularly long hours, roughly 10+ a day or 50+ a week, being overworked, or working 6 or 7 days a week. Often paired with complaints about pay for the extra time.
   This dimension is DURATION, not intensity: "overworked", "worked to death", "no days off" stated as overwork belong here; "too much work" or "heavy workload" are job control instead.
   "No overtime" or "will not pay overtime" without a statement of long hours is dimension 4.
   Vague "tiring hours", "exhausting hours", "crazy hours" are dimension 10.

2  inflexible schedule or no autonomy in scheduling
   Hours or days are rigid, time off is hard to get, or breaks are restricted. Includes "mandatory", "have to", "must", "required", "forced", "mandated" applied to hours, days, shifts or overtime, and being on call or "called in".
   "No day off" or "didn't have an off day" without an overwork statement is coded here only.
   Restrictions on down time, breaks, or lunch timing are coded here even when the excerpt also complains of micromanagement.
   Limited vacation DATES are coded here; add dimension 7 only when the AMOUNT of leave is also limited.

3  schedule and hour variability
   Schedules or hours change from week to week, which for hourly workers also means unstable income. Different from dimension 5: hours can vary yet still be announced well in advance.
   "Random hours" is coded here and also 5. "Random days" alone is 5 only, because hours on the working days may be fixed.

4  insufficient hours
   Not enough hours, shifts or overtime opportunities; hours cut; being sent home; wanting more income. Also hours reduced while the same work is still expected.
   A bare "not enough work" or "nothing to do", with no reference to hours, is job control, not this.
   "Not enough hours to do the job" is coded here and also as job-control under-staffing.

5  low predictability and short notice
   The schedule is unpredictable or announced at short notice; on call; demand-driven, smart-scheduling, seasonal, or weather-determined scheduling.

6  undesirable hours or days
   Early mornings, late nights, split or otherwise disliked hours, or having to work weekends or holidays. Often overlaps with dimension 2 when the worker cannot change the assignment.
   Complaints about peak periods ("busy during holidays", "rush hours are the worst") light this for the timing; the intensity belongs to job control.
   "No holiday pay" is a compensation complaint and is not coded at all.

7  lack of paid time off (PTO), paid vacation, or paid sick leave
   Missing or limited PAID leave. The issue is pay while away from work, not permission to go.

8  broad life-work conflict
   An explicit balance or conflict term: work-life balance, work-family conflict, "no personal life", "no family time". Also work hours conflicting with school, classes, children or personal life.
   Assign only when such a term or conflict is explicit; do not infer it from other scheduling complaints.
   When the text also shows the employer will not accommodate the conflict ("unwilling to work with employees", "hard to take off", hours "hard to work around"), add dimension 2.

9  lack of work-from-home or remote work opportunities
   Remote or hybrid not allowed, mandatory in-person attendance, required or monitored office days, return-to-office policies.

10 general complaints
   Refers to scheduling or work time but is too short or vague for a specific dimension: "scheduling", "work time", "the hours were tiring", "very exhausting hours", "crazy hours", bare "time management".

PROS

11 regular hours or no overwork
   Reasonable hours, no excessive overtime, leaving on time or early, clearly bounded work time, or a standard schedule such as Monday to Friday or 9 to 5.

12 flexibility or autonomy in scheduling
   Choosing, adjusting or swapping shifts, setting your own hours, getting time off easily, taking breaks when needed.
   "Time off", "time away from work", "lots of time off", "easy to get time off" are coded here as evidence that time off can be taken, unless the excerpt is explicitly about PAID leave benefits, which is dimension 16. "Flexible PTO" may go to either depending on context.
   "Free time" or "down time" during work is job control, not this.

13 desirable hours or days
   Daytime schedules, hours that fit the reviewer's life, or not having to work nights, early mornings, weekends or holidays.

14 hour consistency
   Stable, regular or consistent hours over time, which also means stable income for hourly workers.

15 hour predictability or advance notice
   Knowing the schedule ahead of time, reliable scheduling, no sudden changes or on-call expectations. Predictability can exist even when hours vary.

16 paid time off (PTO), paid vacation, or paid sick leave
   PAID leave available, including the number of leave days provided.

17 additional hours opportunities
   Enough hours, or available overtime, extra shifts or extra hours, usually valued as extra income ("never had to worry about hours").
   Praise that the job "keeps you busy" or has "plenty of work" is job control, not this. When both are stated, code both.

18 work-life balance
   Explicitly says good work-life balance or a similar term. Do not infer it from other scheduling compliments.

19 work-from-home or remote work opportunities
   Remote or hybrid arrangements available, reduced commuting, flexible work location.

20 overall compliment
   Praises scheduling or hours but is too short or vague for a specific dimension: "good scheduling", "great work hours", "the hours are great".

COMBINATIONS CODED TOGETHER
  mandatory or forced overtime                                  -> 1 and 2
  "have to work Saturdays", forced weekend or holiday work       -> 2 and 6
  on call, or "called in"                                        -> 2 and 5
  random hours                                                   -> 3 and 5   (random DAYS alone -> 5 only)
  seasonal or periodic hour cuts                                 -> 3 and 4
  limited vacation time AND limited dates                        -> 2 and 7
  school or family conflict the employer will not accommodate    -> 8 and 2
  explicit hours such as "8-5" or "set hours"                    -> 14 and 15, plus 11 if clearly a standard daytime schedule
  "Monday to Friday" with no hours                               -> 13, plus 11 if the standard schedule is obvious
  any named absence of nights, weekends or holidays              -> add 13
  a complaint inside a pros excerpt, or the reverse              -> code both the con and the pro dimension

OUTPUT FORMAT
Reply with exactly 20 digits and nothing else, position 1 through 20, 1 if the dimension applies and 0 if it does not. No spaces, no explanation. At least one digit must be 1.
