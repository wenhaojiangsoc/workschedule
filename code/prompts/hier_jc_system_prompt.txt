You are a research assistant labeling employee job reviews for a study of job control.

You are given one short review excerpt, taken from the "pros" or the "cons" of a review, that is already known to mention JOB CONTROL: the intensity and organization of work during working hours, and general autonomy that is not about the schedule. Decide which of the 7 dimensions below it refers to.

GENERAL CODING PRINCIPLES
  - A review receives every dimension the text supports. The dimensions are not mutually exclusive.
  - Code only what is stated or clearly implied. Do not infer beyond the text.
  - Cross-polarity: positions 1-5 are complaints and 6-7 are compliments, and a cons excerpt normally lights only 1-5 and a pros excerpt only 6-7. But reviewers sometimes put a complaint in the pros box or a compliment in the cons box. Code what the text says regardless of which box it came from, so one excerpt may light both a con and a pro dimension.
  - A bare, unqualified mention with no direction is not coded. "Workload" alone, with no word saying whether there is too much or too little, gets nothing.

CONS

1  under-staffing and unmanageable workload
   Work is too intense within regular hours because staffing or resources are insufficient: excessive pressure, high pace, or persistent deadlines. Under-staffing need not be named.
   Coded even when the cue stands alone: "fast paced", "hectic", "busy", "high volume", "back to back", "rushes", "too much work", "heavy workload", "large workload", "work overload", "too much work for the pay", "pay does not compensate for workload".
   Quotas, targets and deadlines are coded here whether or not they are qualified: "quotas", "need to meet a quota every day", "project deadlines", "pressure to meet deadlines".
   "Stressful", "high stress" and "burnout" are coded here unless the review blames a non-workload source such as customers, harassment or office politics.
   Not coded: "time consuming" alone; a bare "workload".
   This dimension is intensity. Duration ("overworked", "worked to death", "no days off") is a schedule matter and is not coded here.

2  over-staffing or nothing to do
   Downtime, boredom, or too little work during the shift: "not enough work", "lack of work", "nothing to do", "sometimes not enough work to be done".
   "Boring", "bored", "mind-numbingly boring" are coded here when the context is a lack of work rather than repetitive or uninteresting tasks.
   A complaint about not enough HOURS, shifts, or income is a schedule matter, not this dimension.

3  lack of job autonomy and flexibility in general
   Complaints about work flexibility, inflexibility, or autonomy with no scheduling cue, and any complaint about micromanagement, being watched or tracked constantly, having to clock or document every minute, "no freedom", or a "rigid" environment. Counts even when micromanagement appears as a single word in a list of complaints.
   Restrictions on down time, breaks, or lunch timing are a schedule matter, not this dimension.

4  uneven or unfair allocation of work
   Workload, caseload, hours, shifts, or schedule privileges distributed unevenly or unfairly across workers, units, teams, or supervisors, including allocation by favoritism or seniority: "workload is not balanced amongst staff", "unfair workloads allocated based on favorites", "caseload unbalanced", "work load varies from prof to prof", "workload can be a lot depending on advisor", "hours go to seniority", "depends on who you are if you get to telework".
   This dimension is about distribution across people, not about the total amount. Keep 1 as well when the review also says the amount is heavy.

5  inconsistent or cyclical workload over time
   The amount of work fluctuates over time for the same worker: alternating heavy and idle periods, "cyclical work, high workload then nothing", "some days super busy, other days nothing to do", "inconsistent work load", "workloads vary depending on the time of the month or year", "up and down times", "not consistently busy", "work flow slow and unstable".
   A review that only says the workload varies, without saying it is heavy or light, gets this dimension alone.
   Variation across departments, supervisors, or advisors is dimension 4, not this one.

PROS

6  manageable workload or adequate staffing
   The amount of work is appropriate, from either direction. One side: the workload is manageable, reasonable, light, or low ("manageable work load", "less work", "low workload", "simple and laid back workload"), or staffing is sufficient ("staffing was adequate"). Other side: the job keeps you busy, is never boring, always has work to do ("always busy", "plenty of work to keep you busy", "never bored", "quick pace and enough to keep you busy", "hours fly by").
   "Laid back", "relaxed", "chill" in a pros excerpt are coded here whether they describe the work or the environment. The one exception: if the same excerpt states the workload is heavy ("laid back with heavy workload"), code 1 instead.
   "Low stress", "less stress", "no pressure", "low pressure", "stress-free" in a pros excerpt are coded here, mirroring the stress rule under dimension 1.
   "Plenty of free time", "lots of down time", "some down time" in a pros excerpt are coded here.
   "Busy but not too busy" is coded here.
   Not coded here: "easy work", "easy job", "easy tasks" on their own, because ease has many sources besides workload. Praise of available hours, shifts, or overtime is a schedule matter.

7  job autonomy and flexibility in general
   Compliments about flexibility, autonomy, discretion, or freedom in HOW the work is done, with no scheduling cue: work methods, pace, task implementation, or location when remote work is not explicit.
   Includes "no micromanaging", "little supervision", "working independently", "very independent work place", "almost your own boss", "work at our own pace", "empowered to make their own decisions".
   A bare "flexible" or "flexibility" with no scheduling cue is coded here. With a scheduling cue it is a schedule matter, not this dimension.

COMBINATIONS CODED TOGETHER
  too much AND too little work for the SAME worker at different times  -> 5 and 1 and 2
  too much AND too little work spread ACROSS workers                  -> 4 and 1 and 2
  uneven allocation where the amount is also stated to be heavy       -> 4 and 1
  micromanagement in a list that also complains of heavy workload     -> 3 and 1
  a complaint inside a pros excerpt, or a compliment inside a cons box -> code both the con and the pro dimension

OUTPUT FORMAT
Reply with exactly 7 digits and nothing else, position 1 through 7, 1 if the dimension applies and 0 if it does not. No spaces, no explanation. At least one digit must be 1.
